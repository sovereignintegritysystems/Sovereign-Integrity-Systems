# Security and Vulnerability Reporting

Sovereign Integrity Systems (SIS) welcomes good-faith reports of security issues affecting its public repositories, public website or other SIS-controlled public interfaces.

## Reporting route

Send reports to **cgust.dev@gmail.com** with the subject prefix:

`[SIS SECURITY]`

Please include only the information needed to understand and reproduce the issue safely:

- affected public surface or repository;
- concise description of the issue and likely impact;
- steps or evidence sufficient to reproduce it without unnecessary exploitation;
- relevant version, commit, URL or timestamp;
- your preferred contact information, or state that you want to remain anonymous.

Do not send passwords, API keys, private customer data, third-party confidential material or unrelated personal information.

## Good-faith boundaries

Please avoid:

- accessing, modifying, destroying or exfiltrating data that is not yours;
- persistence, lateral movement, denial of service or social engineering;
- testing third-party systems merely because SIS references or uses them;
- publishing enabling exploit detail before SIS has had a reasonable opportunity to validate and remediate a report;
- representing an unverified issue as a confirmed vulnerability.

If a report appears to affect a third-party provider, SIS will distinguish the SIS-controlled surface from the provider-controlled surface and route accordingly.

## SIS handling

SIS will attempt to:

1. acknowledge a sufficiently actionable report;
2. establish the exact affected surface and current version;
3. triage severity and evidence without prematurely promoting the claim;
4. route the issue to the correct technical owner;
5. coordinate disclosure where warranted;
6. preserve a correction and effectiveness record.

A report, acknowledgement, patch or advisory is not itself proof that every deployment or downstream system is affected or remediated. Exact scope and evidence control the claim.

## Current status boundary

This policy establishes a public vulnerability-reporting route. It does not claim certification, a mature PSIRT performance history, bug-bounty eligibility, security clearance, safe-harbour terms beyond applicable law, or any particular response-time SLA.

For ordinary company or research contact, use the same address without the security subject prefix.
