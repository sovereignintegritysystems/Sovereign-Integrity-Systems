# Sovereign Integrity Systems — Institutional Web Identity

Status: CURRENT / PUBLIC WEB PRESENTATION DOCTRINE
Adopted: 2026-08-12
Semantic owner: public institutional projection under founder/brand authority
Canonical visual source: `docs/brand/CANONICAL_MARK.md`

## Purpose

The SIS public website is an institutional front door and public profile, not a product-growth landing page. Its presentation must communicate gravity, continuity, evidence discipline and Canadian institutional identity without simulating maturity that has not been evidenced.

## Identity hierarchy

1. **Sovereign Integrity Systems** is the primary institutional identity.
2. **High-Assurance Critical Infrastructure** is the subordinate positioning line.
3. **Sovereign Engines** is the principal platform family beneath the institution.
4. Programmes, research lanes, engineering lanes and future products remain subordinate to the institution layer.

## Canonical mark

The founder-supplied R3 source registered in `docs/brand/CANONICAL_MARK.md` remains the authoritative public-web source rendition. Visible page instances use the browser-safe self-contained vector projection at `assets/brand/sis-canonical-mark-web-r3.svg`.

The former AVIF projection is retired after a real Chrome observation showed substantial lower graphite and teal geometry disappearing. No AVIF or other alpha/lossy transfer may be promoted as the visible canonical projection without real-browser comparison against the founder source.

The mark is used as an identity anchor in the masthead, institutional profile and footer. It is not used as a decorative background motif. Connected-account imagery may corroborate identity continuity but never supersedes the founder-approved mark.

## Public-copy boundary

Brand provenance and control-plane vocabulary belong in documentation, not visible public-page copy. The page must not visibly label the mark with carrier names, hashes, source-state labels or similar implementation vocabulary.

## Visual system

- dark charcoal field;
- graphite structural rules;
- restrained teal accents;
- white and cool-grey typography;
- square edges, rules and registers instead of rounded marketing cards;
- no ornamental gradients, glass effects, animated particles, floating badges or faux control panels;
- no unsupported metrics, partner-logo walls, certification badges or status theatre.

The surrounding page should remain quieter than the mark.

## Institutional composition rules

- institution name first; positioning second;
- restrained but clearly legible mark scale;
- explicit mandate, platform and operating-state register;
- numbered institutional sections with stable semantic names;
- evidence/authority limitations presented as formal notes rather than promotional cards;
- direct contact and provenance without conversion funnels or urgency language;
- historical identity retained only as continuity/provenance, never current authority.

## Technical presentation rules

The institutional page intentionally remains static HTML/CSS with no JavaScript framework, external font dependency, analytics requirement or client-side rendering dependency. Accessibility requirements include semantic landmarks, keyboard-visible focus, useful alt text, responsive layouts and print-safe representation.

The visible mark carrier must be self-contained and browser-conservative. Nested raster-in-SVG, AVIF-alpha and equivalent compatibility-sensitive paths are prohibited for the primary identity unless proven by real browser observation across the supported environment.

## Evidence and rendering rule

Provider/API evidence that HTML or an asset exists is not sufficient proof of visible propagation. Evidence is reported by layer: source verification, projection verification, repository readback, provider deployment/readback, then real-browser visible rendering.

A browser-visible contradiction reopens the defect even when all preceding layers passed. The 2026-08-12 AVIF regression is the canonical example of this rule.

## Estate propagation rule

The canonical binary/web asset remains owned by the public institutional repository. Other SIS repositories reference this canonical source rather than copy or fork the mark. Private institutional and technical repositories may carry currentness/provenance pointers but do not acquire brand authority.
