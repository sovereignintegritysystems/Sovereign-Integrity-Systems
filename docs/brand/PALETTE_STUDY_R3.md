## Decision

The geometry currently carried by `assets/brand/sis-canonical-mark-web-r3.svg` is the sole admitted public-web geometry for the Sovereign Integrity Systems institutional mark. This study does **not** redesign the mark.

The current R3 palette remains canonical pending an explicit founder/brand-authority decision:

- Graphite: `#333C43`
- Deep teal: `#3D8288`
- Cool neutral leaf: `#C2C5C2`

All files under `assets/brand/experimental/` are colour-only studies. Their polygon coordinates are intentionally identical to the active R3 SVG. They may be used for comparison, accessibility/production testing and collateral exploration; they may not replace the website, repository avatar, institutional mark, or public lockups by inference.

## Institutional design requirements

The mark and its lockups should read in this order:

1. enduring institutional seal;
2. protected/system boundary and engineering structure;
3. Canadian institution;
4. only secondarily, an embedded G/C construction.

The surrounding identity system therefore defaults to:

- flat colour, no glow, bevel, gradient or simulated material depth;
- quiet hierarchy and substantial negative space;
- square/rule/register structures rather than rounded startup cards;
- restrained typography with no novelty display face required for recognition;
- no pseudo-certification badges, unsupported partner marks, metrics theatre or invented maturity claims;
- legal suffixes, trademark symbols, domains, titles and contact details only where actually current and appropriate to the artifact;
- the positioning line `High-Assurance Critical Infrastructure` subordinate to the institution name, never competing with it.

Dimension interpretation for this brand decision:

- **S — strategic sovereign leverage:** longevity and fit with Canadian sovereign institutional identity.
- **R — readiness/requirement fit:** compatibility with the admitted mark, current website, documentation and evidence boundaries.
- **A — actionability/currentness:** ability to deploy safely now without creating contradictory identity state.
- **I — integration/reuse:** compatibility across GitHub, web, Drive, Canva, proposals, technical documents, print and product surfaces.
- **D — defensibility/distinction:** institutional distinctiveness, non-generic construction and disciplined claim signalling.
- **M — multiplicity:** usefulness across many recurring channels and contexts rather than one campaign.

Risk modifiers considered: identity fragmentation, Canadian-symbol overstatement, small-size contrast, print/reverse reproduction, accessibility, provider rendering, legal/maturity-claim contamination and rework cost.

### SRAIDM conclusion

P0 remains dominant because changing palette has no sufficiently strong benefit to offset identity fragmentation and migration cost. P1 Boreal is the only challenger close enough to justify real-context testing. P2 Canadian Signal is useful as a bounded comparison or possibly a future ceremonial/public-affairs variant, but should not become primary without a deliberate brand-authority act. P3/P4 are useful contextual studies, not replacement recommendations.

## Red-team findings

1. **Generated-brand drift:** AI-generated boards can make a visually attractive lookalike appear authoritative. Mitigation: geometry hashes/source chain and explicit non-canonical variant directory.
2. **Maple-leaf overstatement:** bright red can dominate the mark and make SIS read as generic Canadian contractor/government branding. Mitigation: current neutral leaf remains primary; red stays experimental.
3. **Startup visual drift:** gradients, glow, rounded cards, faux dashboards and tech ornament weaken institutional credibility. Mitigation: flat fields, rules, negative space and quiet hierarchy.
4. **Legal-status contamination:** old boards included speculative `Inc.`, `Ltd.`, domains, email and trademark/legal statements. Mitigation: current legal/contact facts are populated from authoritative institutional state, never from visual templates.
5. **Tagline regression:** historical artifacts contain `High-Assurance Digital Infrastructure`. Mitigation: current live lockups use `High-Assurance Critical Infrastructure`; historical images remain archival only.
6. **Geometry-by-colour regression:** colour experimentation can accidentally alter edges, bar width or negative space. Mitigation: every study reuses the exact R3 polygon coordinates.
7. **Single-context optimization:** a palette can look good on one dark screen but fail print, small icons or light government documents. Mitigation: require light/dark, favicon, grayscale, laser-print and ordinary office-printer observations before any palette promotion.
8. **Pseudo-seal risk:** the institutional form must not imply certification, Crown authority, Indigenous-government authority or regulatory status. Mitigation: use as corporate identity only; do not surround it with official-looking certification text or state insignia.

## Admission gate for any future palette change

A palette change is not admitted until all are true:

1. exact R3 geometry identity is conserved;
2. light and dark browser renders are visibly observed;
3. 16/24/32/48 px icon legibility is observed;
4. grayscale and one-colour reproduction are observed;
5. standard office-print and high-quality print samples are compared;
6. contrast/accessibility of accompanying wordmark/tagline is checked separately;
7. Canva and document projections are compared against GitHub/Drive source;
8. current public/legal/contact wording is used;
9. founder/brand authority explicitly promotes a named palette version;
10. GitHub, Drive, Canva and public-currentness records are updated atomically enough to avoid two competing primary identities.

Until that gate closes, **R3 geometry + P0 current palette remains the SIS institutional identity**.
