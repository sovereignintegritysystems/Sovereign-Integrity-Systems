# Sovereign Integrity Systems — Public Publication Boundary

Status: **ACTIVE PUBLICATION POLICY**  
Authority effect: **NONE** — this document constrains publication; it does not create legal, technical, financial, certification, provider, procurement, Indigenous-government or operational authority.

## 1. Role

`sovereignintegritysystems/Sovereign-Integrity-Systems` is the canonical public GitHub publication projection for Sovereign Integrity Systems.

Its job is to make approved public material discoverable and credible without turning GitHub, a website, public biography or marketing language into semantic authority and without exposing proprietary detail merely to demonstrate sophistication.

## 2. Default-deny publication rule

Material is not publishable merely because it exists in GitHub, Google Drive, a chat, a draft, an issue, a PR, a local workspace, laboratory discussion or provider projection.

Publication requires an explicit `PUBLIC_SAFE` classification or equivalent governed public-release decision tied to the exact artifact/currentness being released.

If classification is absent, ambiguous, stale or contradicted by a stronger source, the artifact does not cross the boundary.

## 3. Minimum-sufficient-credibility rule

The public projection must contain enough evidence-bounded information for a reasonable skeptical external reviewer to answer:

- who SIS is and where it operates;
- its current legal/commercial maturity;
- what coherent research and engineering domains it actually works in;
- who currently leads and contributes to it at an institutionally relevant level;
- what real external activity has occurred, with status stated accurately;
- how SIS distinguishes research targets, proposals, source, execution, validation and certification;
- how a legitimate party can make contact.

Publication should stop when those institutional questions can be answered. Extra personal information, internal codenames, exact mechanisms, recipes, thresholds, claim combinations, unpublished proposal details, security architecture or raw evidence does not become PUBLIC_SAFE merely because it might impress a reviewer.

The desired posture is **informative but non-enabling**.

## 4. Layered disclosure architecture

SIS uses three disclosure layers:

1. **Public institutional layer** — identity, maturity, coherent domains, bounded activity, public-safe evidence discipline and a minimal contact path.
2. **Controlled diligence/collaboration layer** — deeper technical, commercial, financial, contractual or research information only for a legitimate decision need and under appropriate NDA/confidentiality, research-security, procurement, IP, data-rights or professional-advice conditions.
3. **File/publish/release layer** — enabling invention detail, source, research data, exact evidence packages or detailed technical methods become public only after an explicit filing, defensive-publication, open-science, release or other governed disclosure decision.

A counterparty's curiosity or prestige does not by itself justify crossing layers.

## 5. Allowed public classes

Subject to currentness and exact claim ceilings, this repository may contain:

- company identity, brand, mission, truthful maturity and public-safe institutional descriptions;
- public product/platform descriptions at a non-enabling level;
- bounded founder and collaborator role descriptions where institutional relevance outweighs privacy cost;
- public research domains, methods classes, defensive publications, standards mappings and technical notes that have crossed the release boundary;
- public grant/proposal summaries where disclosure is allowed and application/proposal status is exact;
- public announcements, opportunities, recruiting and engagement material;
- public release notes, interfaces or code when a separate release decision authorizes them;
- sanitized evidence summaries where the underlying source allows publication and the summary does not inflate the claim.

## 6. Prohibited or restricted classes

Do not commit or publish:

- passwords, API keys, tokens, SSH material, signing keys, recovery codes, seed phrases, credentials or secrets;
- nonpublic personal information, unnecessary personal phone numbers, private addresses or sensitive third-party biography;
- privileged legal advice, executed confidential legal instruments, private corporate records, cap-table implementation, banking, tax, payroll, insurance or nonpublic finance;
- NDA, customer-confidential, partner-confidential, government-confidential, controlled-technical, export-controlled, procurement-sensitive or research-security-sensitive material;
- `NDA_ONLY`, `FILE_FIRST` or `DO_NOT_DISCLOSE` material unless and until the exact artifact is reclassified;
- enabling unfiled invention detail that has not received an explicit file/publish decision;
- raw vulnerability, exploit, red-team, security-control, recovery, credential, infrastructure or operational detail whose release increases risk;
- internal R&D/Engineering control state, raw evidence or provider-operation detail that has not been sanitized and admitted for public release.

Private GitHub visibility is not a secret-management system. Credentials and secrets should not be committed to the internal repository either.

## 7. Indigenous identity / authority boundary

A founder's Indigenous citizenship, identity or ownership may be stated factually where institutionally relevant and supported by the governing source.

It must never be transformed into a claim that SIS:

- speaks for or is endorsed by an Indigenous government, Nation, community or organization;
- has authority to define Indigenous law, governance, cultural protocol or community policy;
- owns community knowledge, culture, data or intellectual property;
- has obtained procurement qualification, partnership, consent or mandate;
- satisfies an ownership/control program solely because an individual identifies as Indigenous.

Those are separate facts requiring separate evidence. Sensitive biography of collaborators or family is not public institutional material absent consent and a real decision need.

## 8. IP / patent-option preservation

Publication is itself an IP event. Before public release of technically substantive material, determine whether the exact content should instead be:

- kept as a trade secret;
- disclosed only under confidentiality;
- filed before publication;
- deliberately defensively published;
- openly released.

Canada's patent grace period is not a universal international safety net. The public repository therefore defaults to non-enabling descriptions of unfiled invention work unless an explicit IP/publication decision authorizes more.

A public research page may establish domain credibility without disclosing the mechanism that creates prospective patent, trade-secret or bargaining value.

## 9. Claim preservation

A public projection may only narrow a claim; it may not strengthen one.

Examples:

- source present ≠ executed;
- executed ≠ integrated;
- integrated ≠ effective;
- internal verification ≠ independent validation;
- proposal submitted ≠ award or contract;
- research discussion ≠ partnership or endorsement;
- draft invention material ≠ patent filing or patent pending;
- standards orientation ≠ certification;
- provider success ≠ semantic authority;
- Indigenous identity ≠ Indigenous-government mandate;
- simulation ≠ observed outcome.

Missing evidence remains `UNOBSERVED`.

## 10. Promotion path

The normal promotion path is:

1. identify the authoritative internal source and exact current version;
2. determine the external audience and decision the disclosure must support;
3. determine disclosure classification and legal/contractual/IP/research-security restrictions;
4. remove unnecessary personal, enabling, security-sensitive, procurement-sensitive and internal-control detail without changing supported meaning;
5. preserve source provenance and claim ceiling;
6. test the public artifact from skeptical academic, government/procurement, customer/partner, investor/funder, competitor/attacker, Indigenous-governance and privacy perspectives as applicable;
7. review the public diff as a public artifact, not as a private mirror;
8. admit it to this repository;
9. read back provider state and record currentness where the internal control architecture requires it.

No automatic private-to-public synchronization is authorized by default.

## 11. Adversarial publication test

Before a material public expansion, ask at least:

- Does this fact materially improve the external credibility or decision the page must support?
- Could the same credibility be achieved with a less enabling statement?
- Could a competitor reconstruct a useful mechanism, claim combination, research plan or commercial strategy from it?
- Could a hostile actor use it for social engineering, physical targeting, credential discovery or security analysis?
- Could a lawyer, tax authority, procurement officer, scientist, regulator or journalist reasonably read it as a stronger legal/commercial/evidence claim than SIS can prove?
- Does it reduce future patent, trade-secret, bargaining, financing, procurement, research-security or estate optionality?
- Does it expose another person's biography/contact information without a necessary institutional reason and consent?

If the answer creates material downside without corresponding decision value, narrow or withhold the disclosure.

## 12. GitHub Actions exclusion

GitHub Actions is outside the current SIS architecture. Publication does not require or consume GitHub-hosted workflows, checks, runs, logs, artifacts, runners, billing state, quotas or conclusions. None of those states may satisfy or block publication, review, evidence, currentness, release or claim status.

GitHub source hosting remains separate from GitHub Actions.

## 13. Demotion and correction

If public material becomes stale, overbroad, unsafe, superseded or inconsistent with a stronger source, correct or withdraw it promptly. Historical public artifacts may be retained where useful, but must be clearly marked as historical/superseded when current interpretation could otherwise be misleading.

## 14. Boundary owners

The public repository does not own the underlying facts. Company-truth, technical, research, engineering, legal, financial, evidence, provider and Indigenous/community relationship states remain owned by their exact governed sources. This repository owns only the public publication projection and its publication-state currentness.
