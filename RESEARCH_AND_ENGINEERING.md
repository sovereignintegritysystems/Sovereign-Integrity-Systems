# Sovereign Integrity Systems — Research & Engineering

This page is a public-safe map of current research and engineering activity. It establishes institutional scope; it is not a release of unpublished enabling inventions and does not claim experimental effectiveness, certification, patentability or freedom to operate.

## High-assurance critical infrastructure

SIS researches and develops systems intended for environments where correctness, provenance, continuity, bounded authority and reconstructable evidence matter. Current public-safe areas include high-assurance Rust, deterministic/bounded execution, cryptography and post-quantum migration, DDIL/offline operation, formal/adversarial assurance, evidence semantics, scientific provenance and standards/applicability research.

## Materials science and measurement

Current materials-science work is organized around **measurement questions rather than product-performance claims**. Public-safe topics include:

- bonded-interface chemical/physical characterization;
- conventional FTIR and infrared spectromicroscopy method selection;
- spatially resolved spectroscopy where ordinary bench characterization is insufficient;
- environmental/moisture/thermal conditioning as controlled nuisance or perturbation variables;
- polymer, cellulose, coating, corrosion and harsh-environment material questions;
- coupling spectroscopy to microscopy, diffraction, surface analysis, tribology or mechanical testing only when the surviving hypothesis requires it;
- predeclared stop/continue criteria and exact separation of bench, facility, synchrotron and field evidence.

SIS uses a Saskatchewan-first facility-routing rule: answer the claim with the least-complex credible local method first, then escalate to specialized Saskatchewan, interprovincial or national infrastructure only when the surviving question requires greater sensitivity, calibration, independence or access to a unique instrument.

## Vibroacoustics and structural dynamics

Current public-safe vibroacoustic research examines whether controlled mechanical/acoustic excitation and synchronized sensing can distinguish changes in a physical transfer or coupling state under repeatable laboratory perturbations.

The early evidence ladder is intentionally non-human and bench-first: layered mechanical/phantom fixtures, controlled excitation, accelerometer/contact-acoustic observation, known positioning/loading perturbations, raw synchronized acquisition, transfer-function/repeatability analysis and explicit nuisance controls. Human, clinical or implant work is not required to answer the first causal physics questions and is not implied by this research description.

Relevant capability areas include vibration excitation/control, modal and transfer-function measurement, acoustic sensing, accelerometry, dynamic modelling, finite-element analysis, controlled loading and calibrated vibration/acoustic metrology.

## Model-based physical engineering

SIS is researching how engineering meaning survives movement between CAD, model-based definition, metrology and physical evidence. Public-safe themes include:

- semantic preservation across CAD/STEP/AP242/QIF/IFC-style exchanges;
- separation of geometric similarity from authoritative product-definition equivalence;
- as-designed / as-built / as-measured reconciliation with explicit uncertainty;
- digital threads for fabrication, inspection, repair and maintenance;
- adversarial cases where files look visually correct while units, frames, datums, tolerances, material/process data or revision authority have changed;
- DDIL/offline model authority and later reconciliation.

## Harsh-environment and wildfire physical evidence

SIS also researches physical evidence for harsh Saskatchewan and wildfire-relevant conditions: heat, smoke, moisture, freeze/thaw, salts, vibration, wind, corrosion and material aging. Research may include coupons, fixtures, sensors, fluid/nozzle tests, smoke/media characterization, mechanical durability and instrumented field protocols.

Simulation and literature can prioritize these questions but cannot substitute for physical observations where a physical-performance claim is at issue.

## Measurement and facility strategy

Scarce facilities are treated as evidence resources rather than prestige targets. SIS distinguishes the **minimum decision core** from the **prepared experimental reserve**: inexpensive matched specimens or observations may be staged in advance when reacquiring a facility could take months, while acquisition beyond the core is still gated by predeclared stop/continue criteria.

Typical escalation logic is:

1. commodity or in-house bench falsifier;
2. Saskatchewan university/applied-research or commercial laboratory;
3. specialized interprovincial or national laboratory/metrology facility;
4. synchrotron, clinical, regulated or other scarce infrastructure only when its unique capability is necessary.

This approach is intended to reduce both forms of waste: expensive access spent discovering a cheap bench failure, and cheap preparation savings that cause an avoidable second access cycle.

## Evidence boundary

A research program appearing here means the question is actively governed and considered suitable for public disclosure. It does **not** mean that SIS has demonstrated a product claim, obtained an external endorsement, completed independent validation, satisfied a regulatory requirement, filed a patent or established freedom to operate.

Negative and null results remain valid research outcomes. Missing physical or external evidence remains unobserved rather than being converted into PASS or FAIL.
