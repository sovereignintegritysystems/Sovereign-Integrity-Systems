How an engagement starts

We prefer to do useful work before asking you for a large data package.

Where lawful public or client-authorized information is sufficient, SIS can first reconstruct the question, identify the strongest evidence, map plausible routes and state what would falsify the analysis. A public-source report can be a complete deliverable on its own.

If internal information would materially improve the result, the next step is to request only the specific figures or facts that change the analysis—for example a capacity figure, cost band, current pipeline overlap or exact technical constraint—rather than broad access by default.

The resulting work may be a completed report, a customized roadmap, bounded implementation, recurring currentness work or another explicitly scoped form. Commercial terms are set case by case from the scope, evidence, client value, risk allocation and applicable legal or professional constraints. Evidence boundary. Estimated opportunity is not guaranteed value. A proposal is not a contract. Delivery is not proof of an economic outcome. Where compensation depends on an outcome, the baseline, attribution and measurement basis must be agreed before that outcome is used commercially.

Good first questions

A useful inquiry can be short.

“Are we missing something?”Ask us to map a market, program, procurement route, partner surface, requirement or technical dependency.
“Where is the waste?”Give us a bounded process or technical system and the result you care about; we can identify what evidence is needed to test the economics.
“Can this claim be trusted?”We can separate source, assumptions, execution evidence, currentness and unresolved validation.
“What should we do next?”We can turn a complicated evidence surface into a ranked roadmap with explicit dependencies and disconfirmers.
